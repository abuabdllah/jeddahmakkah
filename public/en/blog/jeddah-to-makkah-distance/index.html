Jeddah to Makkah Distance

Quick answer: Makkah is about 80 km (50 miles) from central Jeddah, and about 100 km (62 miles) from King Abdulaziz International Airport to Masjid al-Haram. Below you'll find the distance from different parts of Jeddah, travel times by every type of transport, and tips for the journey.

Roads between Jeddah and Makkah

Two main roads connect the cities:

  • Haramain Expressway: the fastest and most used road. It passes close to King Abdulaziz Airport, so it's the natural choice from the airport and north Jeddah.
  • Old Makkah road: convenient for some central and southern Jeddah districts, and sometimes a good alternative when the expressway is busy.

There are no road tolls between Jeddah and Makkah, and fuel stations and service areas are available along the way.

Best time to travel from Jeddah to Makkah

  • Quietest: early mornings on weekdays.
  • Busiest: Thursday evenings, before and after Friday prayer, and during Ramadan — especially before iftar and in the last ten nights.
  • The last few kilometres: traffic around the Haram builds up before and after prayers, so the final stretch can take longer than expected.
  • Summer: daytime temperatures are very high — an air-conditioned car and travelling early or late make the trip much more comfortable.

How long by train?

The Haramain high-speed train links Makkah, Jeddah, King Abdulaziz Airport, King Abdullah Economic City and Madinah. The ride itself between Jeddah and Makkah is shorter than driving, but you need to add the time to reach the station in Jeddah and then get from Makkah station to your hotel. Distances inside Makkah: from the Haram to the holy sites

Once you reach Makkah, these are the approximate distances from Masjid al-Haram to places pilgrims often visit:

  • Masjid Aisha (Al-Tan'eem): about 7–8 km — the nearest point outside the Haram boundary, used by residents of Makkah to enter ihram for Umrah.
  • Mina: about 5–7 km.
  • Muzdalifah: about 10 km.
  • Arafat (Jabal al-Rahmah area): about 20 km.
  • Al-Aziziyah district: a few kilometres — a popular area for more affordable hotels.

Driving yourself from Jeddah to Makkah

If you rent a car in Jeddah, keep in mind:

  • Traffic drives on the right, and speed cameras are common on the highways — stick to the posted limits.
  • Parking near the Haram is very limited; many drivers use the large car parks on the edge of the central area and continue by bus or taxi.
  • Some roads around the Haram close to private cars at prayer times and in peak seasons.
  • Navigation apps work well, but road layouts near the Haram change often during projects — follow the signs.

For most visitors, especially on a first trip, a driver who knows the drop-off points is less stressful than driving and parking yourself.

Important: entering Makkah

Entry to the city of Makkah is restricted to Muslims, and there are checkpoints on the roads into the city. Pilgrims coming for Umrah should also make sure they hold any permit required at the time of travel, such as a booking on the Nusuk app.

Ihram when travelling from Jeddah

Pilgrims flying in pass the miqat before landing and usually enter ihram on the plane. Residents of Jeddah who want to perform Umrah enter ihram from where they are in Jeddah. More in our Umrah arrival guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the distance between Jeddah and Makkah?

About 80 km (50 miles) from central Jeddah to Makkah, and about 100 km (62 miles) from Jeddah Airport to Masjid al-Haram.

How long does it take from Jeddah to Makkah by car?

Around 60–75 minutes from central Jeddah in normal traffic, and 60–90 minutes from the airport.

How far is Makkah from Jeddah Airport?

About 100 km to Masjid al-Haram, usually 60–90 minutes by car.

Are there tolls on the Jeddah–Makkah road?

No, there are no road tolls between Jeddah and Makkah.

What is the fastest way from Jeddah to Makkah?

The train is fastest for the ride itself, but door to door a private car is often as fast or faster, especially with luggage.

Can non-Muslims travel to Makkah?

No, entry to Makkah is restricted to Muslims, with checkpoints on the roads into the city.
